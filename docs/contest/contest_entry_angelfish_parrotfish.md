# Morning Commute at the Seahives

A French angelfish and a parrotfish pass each other in front of the "seahives," the hexagonal concrete modules of the Wahoo Bay artificial reef in Pompano Beach, Florida, while grunts hover around the structure. Every fish is boxed and named by an artificial-intelligence system that watches this reef around the clock.

The seahives were placed to give fish shelter close to shore. To learn who actually uses them, two underwater cameras stream live video to a computer at Florida Atlantic University, which checks every frame, finds each fish and suggests a species. Volunteers then confirm or correct its guesses online, and the system has logged hundreds of millions of sightings since spring.

No photographer took this picture. The camera saved it automatically just before 7 a.m. on a June morning because the scene was unusually busy, and a second AI later picked it from about a million saved frames as one of the most striking. The species names were checked by hand.
