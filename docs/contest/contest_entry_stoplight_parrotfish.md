# Stoplight at Midday

A stoplight parrotfish glides past the "Seahives," the hexagonal concrete modules of the Wahoo Bay artificial reef in Pompano Beach, Florida, while a school of smallmouth grunts drifts around the structure. Every fish is boxed and named by an artificial-intelligence system that watches this reef around the clock.

The Seahives were placed to give fish shelter close to shore. To learn who actually uses them, two underwater cameras stream live video to a server at Florida Atlantic University, which checks every frame, finds each fish and suggests a species. Volunteers then confirm or correct its guesses online, and the system has logged hundreds of millions of sightings since spring.

No photographer took this picture. The camera saved it automatically at 12:40 on an August afternoon because the scene was unusually busy, and a second AI later picked it from about a million saved frames as one of the most striking. The species names were checked by hand.

---

Camera: Axis pan-tilt-zoom network camera in a View Into The Blue "Octopus" underwater housing, permanently mounted on the SEAHIVE artificial reef at Wahoo Bay, Pompano Beach, Florida.

Lighting: natural daylight only, no strobes or video lights. Image taken at 12:40 p.m. in about 5 feet of water.

Capture: the camera streams continuous H.264 video at 1280 × 720 pixels and 30 frames per second over the city's network to a server at Florida Atlantic University. Software on that server decodes every frame, runs an AI fish detector and species classifier on an NVIDIA H200 GPU, and automatically saves frames of interest as JPEGs. This frame was one of those automatic saves, not a shutter press.

Processing: the boxes and species labels were drawn by software from the detector's output after the species names were checked by hand. The image was later cropped, enlarged and colour-enhanced for display, so it exceeds the camera's native resolution and is more saturated than the original frame.
